2. Core concepts

2.8 Contraction and dilation

When soil is sheared, its particles rearrange. A loose arrangement may allow particles to move into the available void spaces, producing contraction. A dense arrangement may require particles to climb over one another, producing dilation. NorSand describes the plastic volume-change tendency using plastic dilatancy,

Dp=dεvpdεqp

In the implementation used here, plastic dilatancy is expressed as

Dp=Mi−η

where Mi is the stress ratio at the image point and η=q⁄p′ is the current stress ratio. With the compression-positive convention used here, η<Mi⇒Dp>0 represents plastic contraction, η=Mi⇒Dp=0, represents zero plastic dilatancy, and η>Mi⇒Dp<0, represents plastic dilation. A soil described as dense does not necessarily dilate from the very beginning of loading. Dense specimens may initially contract, reach a transformation point at which Dp=0, and then begin to dilate. Similarly, contraction and strain-softening should not be treated as identical concepts. The response depends on the evolving stress ratio, state parameter, yield surface, and loading path.
