2. Core concepts

2.12 Drained and undrained loading

This website initially presents four conventional triaxial compression cases:

  • dense drained;
  • loose drained;
  • dense undrained;
  • loose undrained;

Drained Loading

In a drained test, pore water is allowed to enter or leave the specimen and excess pore-water pressure is not sustained. The specimen can therefore change volume during shearing. A specimen that is denser than critical state may contract initially and then dilate, often developing a peak deviator stress before softening toward critical state. A specimen that is looser than critical state generally remains contractive and commonly hardens toward critical state without developing a pronounced peak above the critical-state strength. For the idealized drained simulations presented here, excess pore-water pressure is taken as, Δu=0.

Undrained Loading

In an undrained test, water is not allowed to drain from the specimen. For a saturated soil, the total volume remains approximately constant. However, the soil skeleton may still have a tendency to contract or dilate. A contractive tendency produces positive excess pore-water pressure and reduces mean effective stress. A dilative tendency potentially produces negative excess pore-water pressure and increases mean effective stress. For conventional triaxial compression at constant total confining pressure, excess pore-water pressure is calculated as:

Δu=q−q03−(p′−p0′)

For an initially isotropic condition with q0=0,

Δu=p0′+q3−p′

The website visualizations include a separate plot of excess pore-water pressure against axial strain for undrained simulations. NorSand parameters are generally calibrated together using several triaxial tests rather than fitting each parameter independently from a single response curve. A reliable calibration should reproduce the stress–strain response, the volumetric or pore-pressure response, and the effects of initial state and confining pressure.
