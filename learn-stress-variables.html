2. Core concepts

2.1 Stress variables used in triaxial loading

This website initially focuses on conventional axisymmetric triaxial compressionA laboratory test in which a soil specimen, usually cylindrical, is loaded under controlled stress and drainage conditions. In this test, the specimen is subjected to a major principal effective stress, σ1′, in the axial direction and two equal minor principal effective stresses, σ2′=σ3′, in the radial directions. The mean effective stress is:

p′=σ1′+2σ3′3

and the deviator stress is

q=σ1′−σ3′

Their ratio is called the stress ratio:

η=qp′

The stress ratio provides a convenient measure of how strongly the specimen is being sheared relative to the pressure confining it. The soil does not respond directly to total stress alone. Its strength and deformation are governed mainly by effective stress, the stress carried through the soil skeleton after accounting for pore-water pressure. This distinction becomes especially important in undrained loading. When water cannot escape, pore-water pressure may increase, causing the effective stress to decrease even though the externally applied total stress continues to increase. In tensor formA compact way to write stress relationships using tensor notation, the effective-stress principle may be written as

σ′=σ−uI

where σ is the total stress tensorA mathematical representation of the total stress on a soil element in all directions at a point, before subtracting pore-water pressure, u is pore-water pressure, and I is the identity tensor.
