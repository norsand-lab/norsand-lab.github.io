1. Introduction to NorSand

1.1 Understanding NorSand

Soils may look simple, but their behavior can change dramatically depending on how densely the particles are packed, their current effective stress Stress carried by the soil skeleton. For saturated soil, it is total stress minus pore-water pressure, whether water is allowed to drain, and how they are loaded. A relatively dense sand may develop a dilative response and a peak strength, whereas a relatively loose sand generally tends to contract. Under undrained loading, this contractive tendency can generate positive excess pore-water pressurePressure of water in the soil pores. Changes in pore-water pressure directly affect effective stress, reduce effective stress, and, in sufficiently loose states, lead to strain-softeningReduction in deviator stress or mobilized strength with continued strain after a peak or static liquefactionUndrained loss of strength in a loose, contractive soil as pore-water pressure builds up and effective stress decreases. The same soil can therefore respond very differently under different initial density and loading conditions. Critical State Soil Mechanics, commonly abbreviated as CSSM, provides a framework for understanding these different responses. Its central idea is that a given soil has a characteristic critical-state relationship. Samples of that soil may start from different densities and effective stress levels and may follow different stress–strain paths, but continued monotonic shearing tends to move their states toward the critical state defined for that material. At the critical state, the soil continues to shear without further change in mean effective stress, deviator stressShear-related stress measure, q, or volume.

NorSand is a constitutive model developed by Jefferies (1993) for sands and other particulate materials within the CSSM framework. A constitutive model is a set of mathematical relationships used to predict how a material responds to loading. In geotechnical engineering, these relationships connect changes in stress to changes in strain, volume, strength, and pore-water pressure. Rather than treating loose and dense soils as fundamentally different materials, NorSand represents both within the same critical-state framework. Their different responses arise mainly from the soil's current density, effective stress, and stress-stateThe current combination of stresses acting on the soil, commonly described here by p′ and q position relative to its critical state line. Using this information, NorSand calculates how stress, strain, volume change, dilatancy, and strength evolve during loading. NorSand has become particularly important in mine-tailings engineering because it can represent both dilative behavior and strongly contractive behavior associated with static liquefaction. However, the ideas behind NorSand are useful well beyond tailings. They provide a general way to understand how granular soils evolve from their initial condition toward critical state.
